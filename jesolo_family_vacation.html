Getting there: airport → apartment

Flying into Venice Marco Polo Airport (VCE) — about 33 km / 35–45 minutes from your apartment. Here are your three main options; prices and schedules are indicative, so double-check before you fly.

🛬VCE Airport
🏖️Via Dante Alighieri 98

🚌 ATVO airport bus

~45–70 min · roughly €12–15/adult

Direct coach service between the airport and Jesolo, runs roughly hourly all day. Easiest budget option, but tight with lots of luggage and three kids — check the timetable matches your landing time.

🚕 Taxi / private transfer

~35–45 min · roughly €90–130 fixed

Door-to-door, no transfers. Worth pre-booking a fixed-rate private transfer for a family of 5 with luggage, especially if you land outside normal hours.

🚗 Rental car

~35–45 min

Follow the SR14 Triestina or A4 motorway toward the Jesolo/Caorle exit, then SR43 into Lido di Jesolo. Handy if you want flexibility for beach-day errands or the optional Murano/Burano add-on.

🕓
Your check-in window is 16:00–19:00 on 1 July — an early or midday flight gives you breathing room. Landing later? Message the property in advance about a late check-in.

Best family areas in Jesolo

Lido di Jesolo is organised around several piazzas along its 15 km beach, each with its own character. You're already booked, so here's the lay of the land around your door, plus how the other piazzas compare if you wander further.

📍
Near your apartment (Via Dante Alighieri 98): this address sits toward the quieter western stretch of the street, in the Piazza Milano / Piazza Marconi neighbourhood — based on nearby numbers on the same road, expect somewhere around a 5–15 minute walk to the sand (worth confirming the exact distance with your host on arrival). Da Omar is a few doors down the same street, and La Cambusa is a short walk away in the Piazza Milano square itself — your two most convenient dinner options without needing a bus or bike.
👑 YOUR AREA

🏡 Piazza Milano

Vibe: Residential and calm, mostly local families, low-to-medium noise.

Beach: Long, wide, gentle entry — great for young children.

Evenings: Via Altinate is traffic-free after 20:00, few nightclubs nearby.

🎉 Piazza Mazzini

Vibe: Lively and central, lots of bars and nightlife.

Beach: Well-equipped but crowded in summer; fine for older kids.

Evenings: Via Bafile car-free after 20:00, but noisy late — good for a visit, further from your apartment.

🎡 Piazza Brescia

Vibe: Central and busy, hosts events and the Wheel of Venice.

Beach: Good access but crowded; near Tropicarium Park.

Evenings: Nice for a Ferris-wheel stroll and change of scene — a bus or bike ride from your apartment.

🚌 Piazza Drago

Vibe: Practical, near the bus station, medium noise.

Beach: Close to central beach and amenities but lacks charm.

Evenings: Mainly useful for transport connections, not an evening destination.

Practical family tips

✓
Venice access fee: Applies 8:30–16:00 on 3, 4, 5, 10, 11, 12, 17, 18, 19, 24, 25, 26 July 2026. Your trip is booked for Tue 7 July — a fee-free day, so no charge or pre-registration needed.
✓
Book ahead: Beach sunbeds, restaurant dinners, Caribe Bay tickets, and any airport transfer you want fixed-price.
✓
Transport: ATVO buses run from Jesolo to Punta Sabbioni (for Venice) and to Caorle — scenic, and no parking hassle.
✓
Beach setup: Confirm sunbed access near the apartment or reserve in advance. Ask about towel rental and kids' playgrounds at the beach club.
✓
July heat: This is peak summer — plan beach time before 11:00 and after 16:00, pack sun protection for the kids, keep everyone hydrated, and don't fight a midday siesta.
✕
Avoid: spending evenings in Piazza Mazzini if noise bothers you at night; combining Venice with Murano & Burano in one day; more than one main activity per day.
🐢
Stay calm: slow mornings, afternoon rest, early evenings. Kids aged 5–10 enjoy the beach more than a packed schedule — repeat favourite restaurants instead of chasing something new every night.

🧠 Local hacks (things most tourists miss)

A few money- and time-saving tricks that don't make it into most guidebooks.

1
Your kids don't pay the Venice fee — but still need a QR code. Children under 14 are exempt from the access fee entirely, including on the fee-days above. The catch: everyone (even exempt kids) still has to register online beforehand to get the free exemption QR code, so don't skip that step just because it's "free."
2
The fee only applies 8:30–16:00. Even on a charged date, arriving before 8:30 or after 16:00 means no fee at all. Worth knowing as a backup if your fee-free Tuesday ever has to shift.
3
Refill, don't rebuy, water in Venice. Tap water is safe to drink, and free public fountains ("nasoni") are scattered all over the city. Bring refillable bottles for the kids instead of paying €2–3 a bottle in the summer heat.
4
Walk a few streets back from Piazza San Marco to eat. Restaurants ringing the square mark prices up heavily for the view. The same quality is cheaper just a couple of streets inland — handy since you're already keeping the day simple.
5
Shops close for a few hours midday. Many Italian shops and some supermarkets pause for "riposo" in the early afternoon, including in Jesolo and Caorle. Do souvenir/grocery runs in the morning or after 16:00 rather than right after lunch.
6
Dinner starts late — ask for early seating anyway. Most Italian restaurants don't really get going until 19:30–20:00, which is late for a 5-year-old. Family places like Tempini and La Cambusa are used to early diners; just ask when you book.
